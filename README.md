## Which tier fits your situation

**One blog, no email list.** Core. The feature set covers a complete content site: pages, a blog with drafts and scheduling, categories, an image optimiser, moderated comments, a menu builder, three user roles with 2FA, and the SEO plumbing most themes make you bolt on separately. What you don't get is a subscriber list or campaigns, so the moment a newsletter becomes part of the plan you're on Pro anyway.

**One site that needs to grow an audience or earn from it.** Pro. The subscriber list, open-rate tracking, ad slots and in-dashboard analytics are the features that pay for themselves if you actually use them. Scheduled backups are the ones you only notice when something breaks.

**Two or three sites, or client work.** Business. The extra money buys domains and support length, not features. If you're handing a finished site to a client, this is the first tier where that's permitted at all.

**White-label, SaaS, or resale.** Agency. It's the only tier where you can strip the branding and the only one that allows hosting it for other people.

**Who should skip it.** A five-page brochure site with no blog doesn't need user roles, comment moderation and a form builder; a static site generator or a stock WordPress install does the same job for less. The feature list also shows no plugin marketplace, so if you need an integration that isn't already in the box, custom code injection is the extension route, and that arrives with Pro. Last case: if a full year of support matters to you and you run a single site, the tier that includes 12 months is Business, which means paying for two extra domains to get it.

## Where the licence terms cost you later

The pricing page states that each licence covers a single production domain, with Business at three and Agency unlimited. The summary doesn't spell out staging or local development, so if you keep a staging copy of your site, get that confirmed before you pay rather than after.

This is software you install and run on your own server, so hosting sits outside the licence price. Budget for it separately, along with the email setup your contact form and campaigns will depend on.

Support length is the second difference, and it's the one people skim past. Core is community support only. Pro gives priority tickets with a guaranteed response time for 6 months. Business and Agency both run 12 months, and Business and above add email plus ticket support with a faster SLA. On a single-domain licence, the gap between 6 and 12 months is the most expensive line in this comparison.

Refunds are tight. The vendor describes the product as downloadable and defers to the marketplace refund policy. The store's own purchase notes are blunt about digital goods: sales are final once delivered unless local law or a specific after-sale policy says otherwise. Treat the live demo as your trial period.

One more detail worth knowing. The store states that everything it sells is authorised by the original developer, and that if you lose a licence code you should contact the developer first. Save the code somewhere you'll find it in a year and register it as soon as the order clears.
